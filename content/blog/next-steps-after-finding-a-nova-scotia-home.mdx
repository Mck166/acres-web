Finding a Nova Scotia home you like is the fun part. The next part is a short, unromantic sequence: confirm you are buying the right lot, get a lender and a [lawyer](/blog/real-estate-lawyers-nova-scotia) ready, view it like an inspector, write an offer with conditions you are willing to use, and close. People get hurt when they treat the heart icon as a plan.

If you have not already, [create an account](/signup) so the listing stays in [favorites](/blog/how-to-browse-homes-with-acres) while you make calls. Keep browsing [properties](/properties) and the [map](/map) until this one still wins. Then stop shopping long enough to do the work.

## 1. Make sure it is still the house you think it is

Re-read the listing the boring way: heat, PID, taxes, water, sewer, “as is.” Confirm the pin on the [map](/map). If the lot, the shore, or the acreage is the point, look the parcel up. A lawyer will do a real search later. Your job now is to catch the obvious mismatch — the photo of a neighbour’s water, the shared driveway, the condo that was described like a house.

If you have not chosen a lawyer yet, choose one who does Nova Scotia real estate every week, not a relative who did a closing in 2011. Title, trust deposits, and deed recording are local. Get their name before you need a 24-hour turnaround on a clause.

## 2. Put money and insurance in motion

If you do not have a current pre-approval, get one. [CMHC](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/) material is useful if you are putting less than 20 percent down. Your broker or lender will still underwrite the actual property: well and septic, rural appraisal, condo documents, unusual construction.

Call a home insurance broker with the address. Oil tanks, wood stoves, coastal lots, and old wiring are easier to discover now than three days before closing. If nobody will insure it on terms you can live with, you do not have a deal, you have a crush.

Know where the deposit will come from. In this province the deposit is often due quickly after acceptance, held in trust. It is not a closing cost, but it is cash on a clock.

## 3. View it again, with a checklist

The first viewing is emotional. The second should be nosy. Run water. Open the panel if you are allowed. Look at the basement after you have been in the attic, or the other way around. Walk the grade. Ask where the septic tank is. Ask the age of the roof and the oil tank. If it is a condo, ask about the reserve, pets, rentals, and what the fees include.

Bring someone unsentimental if you can. Take notes in the same order on every house so you can compare. If you are from away, do not skip the in-person visit because the video was charming. Video does not smell a basement.

Book a [home inspection](/blog/home-inspections-nova-scotia) only after you have a firm enough intention to spend the fee — usually after an accepted offer with an inspection condition. You can still walk. That is what the condition is for.

## 4. Hire representation you understand

If you are using a REALTOR, you should already know whether you are a client of the brokerage and what the buyer brokerage agreement says. Confirm they are licensed with the [Nova Scotia Real Estate Commission](https://nsrec.ns.ca/). If you are not using one, you still need a lawyer, and you still need to understand you are unrepresented in the trade.

Do not write an offer on a napkin. Do not accept “we’ll sort conditions later.” [Buying a house in Nova Scotia](/blog/how-to-buy-a-house-in-nova-scotia) is a paper process with dates. Financing, inspection, and sometimes the sale of your current home are the usual brakes. Use them if you need them. Waive them only as a clear financial decision.

Your lawyer should see the offer structure, especially on condos, rural land, waterfront, or anything “as is.” That is not delay. That is the job.

## 5. Offer, then actually use the conditions

An accepted offer is the start of due diligence, not the end of shopping anxiety. In the condition window:

- Inspection and any specialists (septic, well test, WETT, oil tank)
- Financing and appraisal
- Insurance bindable on closing
- Condo status documents if applicable
- Lawyer’s preliminary look at obvious title or description issues

If the inspection finds a failed field or a roof at the end of its life, you renegotiate, ask for work, or end the deal under the condition. That is not bad faith. That is why the clause exists.

Ask [PVSC](https://www.pvsc.ca/) and the tax bill to line up with what you were told. Ask the lawyer about closing adjustments so you are not surprised by property tax or oil in the tank.

## 6. Close, then keep the file

Closing runs through the lawyer’s trust account. You get keys when the deed and the lender are satisfied. Budget a buffer for the first week: oil delivery, well pump, snow, a locksmith, the discovery that the previous owner took the garden shed that was not in the inclusions.

Keep the survey if you have one, the septic records, the tank invoice, and the water tests. The next buyer will ask. You will also want them the first time something breaks.

## If you are still deciding between two homes

Stay on Acres long enough to be honest. Open [listings](/properties) again, look at the [map](/map), and see whether the second favorite is actually a different life. [Sign up](/signup) if you have not, so both addresses sit in one favorites list instead of a text thread of screenshots.

Then pick. Nova Scotia houses do not become easier to buy because you hovered. They become easier when you put professionals on a calendar. Find the home, then do the next unglamorous steps in order. That is how people actually take keys in this province.
