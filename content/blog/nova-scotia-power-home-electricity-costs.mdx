Electricity is part of owning a Nova Scotia home whether you heat with a heat pump, oil, wood, or baseboard. [Nova Scotia Power](https://www.nspower.ca) is the utility most households deal with for the wires, the meter, and the bill. Rates, riders, and programs change. This article will not invent a cents-per-kWh number for you. Open NS Power’s own pages for the tariff that applies to a residential account today, then judge the house in front of you by how it uses power, not by a blog’s outdated figure.

While you shop, look at [listings](/properties) and the [map](/map) with heat source in the same glance as price. [Save the houses](/blog/how-to-browse-homes-with-acres) whose systems you understand. [Create an account](/signup) if you want that list on more than one device.

[Buying a house here](/blog/how-to-buy-a-house-in-nova-scotia) without a winter electricity plan is how people meet January as a surprise.

## What the utility actually bills

A typical residential bill is energy (kilowatt-hours used) plus fixed charges and whatever riders and adjustments are in force. Demand, time-of-day, and optional programs exist for some customers; whether they help you depends on how you live and what NS Power currently offers. The only reliable source for those details is [nspower.ca](https://www.nspower.ca).

What you can know without a rate card:

- A leaky, electric-baseboard house will use more kWh than a tight house with a heat pump, all else equal
- A big hot-water tank, a heated garage, and a workshop welder are not rounding errors
- “Equalized billing” or budget plans smooth the year; they do not shrink the kWh
- Service in your name, a security deposit, and the date of the last meter read are account issues, not listing poetry

Ask the seller for a year of bills, not a verbal “it’s not bad.” One January without context is as misleading as one July. If the seller heats with oil or wood and uses electricity for lights and the well pump, their bill is not your bill if you plan to run two heat pumps and work from home.

[Heating costs in Nova Scotia](/blog/heating-costs-nova-scotia) deserve their own pass because heat is usually the large variable. Electricity is the meter that often carries that heat, or the backup when the oil truck is late.

## Heat pumps, baseboards, and mixed systems

Many Nova Scotia houses now have ductless heat pumps. They can cut the kWh needed to stay warm compared with old baseboards, when they are sized and installed for this climate and when the house is not a wind tunnel. They do not make a 1960s bungalow with no insulation cheap. They also do not always heat the basement, the mudroom, or the far bedroom. Ask what still has baseboard, what has a wood stove, and what happens at −15°C.

Oil plus a heat pump is a common mix. The pump does the shoulder seasons; the oil boiler or furnace covers the deep cold — or the reverse, depending on how the house was set up. Your electricity bill and your oil bill have to be read together. Looking at only one is how people congratulate themselves in October.

Electric thermal storage, space heaters, and “we close off the upstairs” are not heating systems. They are coping. Price the house as if you will heat the whole thing, because you will, eventually.

If the property is rural, the well pump, the septic pump, and the backup generator are part of the electrical story. A generator that has never been test-run is a decoration. Outage habits on the Eastern Shore, the South Shore, and inland are not the same as a peninsula block with underground feed, but even Halifax sees storms. Ask neighbours, not only the listing.

Federal and provincial efficiency information changes. [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca) and [novascotia.ca](https://novascotia.ca) are the places to look for current rebate language, not a screenshot in a Facebook group. Programs have rules, dates, and equipment lists. A heat pump that does not qualify is still a heat pump; it just is not a rebate.

## What to check on a viewing

You do not need to be an electrician. You do need to be nosy.

- Find the electrical panel. Is it a modern breaker panel with space, or a full board with a story?
- Ask about the service size if you plan a heat pump, an EV charger, or a shop
- Note electric baseboards in rooms the heat pump cannot see
- Ask how hot water is made: electric tank, oil, or heat-pump water heater
- In a condo, ask whether heat and common-area power are in the fee or on your meter
- On land or a new build, ask how far the line is and who pays to extend it — NS Power will quote; a seller’s shrug will not

Knob-and-tube, old fuse panels, and amateur additions show up in this province’s older stock. An inspector flags them; an insurer may have opinions too. Budget for an electrician if the panel looks like a museum.

On older houses this is often the silent line item: not the pretty kitchen, the service upgrade that makes the heat pump legal and insurable.

## Connecting, closing, and the first month

On closing, electricity does not magically jump to your name. You arrange an account with Nova Scotia Power, give them the closing date, and make sure the seller is not leaving you without power because someone assumed the other person called. Same for oil if that is still in the mix.

The first bill may include a partial period, a deposit, or a catch-up if the house sat vacant. Equalized billing, if you want it, is a conversation with the utility after you have usage history — again, on their terms, on their site.

If you are comparing two houses, compare envelope and systems first, then look up today’s residential information on [nspower.ca](https://www.nspower.ca). Do not multiply a blogger’s old rate by a square footage guess and call it a budget. kWh depend on how the house is built and how you live. The tariff depends on the regulator and the date.

[Browse homes](/properties) with the heat source visible in your notes. Use the [map](/map) if you are comparing a tight newer house in the suburbs with an older place on the shore: the wind and the exposure are part of the electric story too.

Keep a year of your own bills when you have them. The next buyer will ask, and you will already know the answer.
